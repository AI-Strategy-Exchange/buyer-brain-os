# Prompts

 Verbatim prompt text for `SKILL.md`. Do not paraphrase these; the wording is load-bearing.

## Literature Casting

*Stage 1. Cast five characters, stop for a human choice, then build one ordinary Tuesday.*

```
MY AVATAR DOC: [paste the whole thing, however rough]
THE PROBLEM I SOLVE FOR THEM: [one line]

STEP 1. Before anything else.
Read my avatar doc and tell me what ordinary Tuesday it implies. Four or
five lines, hour by hour. Don't improve it. If the doc is thin or generic,
that Tuesday should come out thin and generic, and you should say so
plainly. Save it. We're coming back to it.

STEP 2. The casting.
You've read everything. Not just the canon, but the minor novels, the
supporting characters, the plays nobody assigns.

Find 5 fictional characters who live my buyer's inner life: the ambition,
the fear, the self-image, the daily grind.

Match on inner life, not job title. Don't hand me a salesman because my
buyer sells things. Supporting characters are fair game and often better:
the protagonist gets the arc, the minor character gets the unguarded
moment. No more than one from the most obvious shelf.

For each:
1. Who they are, what work they're from, one line on the situation
2. What the author understood about this kind of person that a survey or
   focus group would never surface
3. What they want but would never say out loud. First person, as a
   sentence they'd only say at 2am
4. What they fear but would never admit. First person, same rule
5. The scene that shows it. Describe it. If you quote, quote exactly and
   mark it as a quote. If you're unsure of the wording, paraphrase and say
   so. Never invent a line.

Then the common thread. Push past the first abstraction. "They want to be
seen" is not an answer. Go until you hit something specific enough that it
would be WRONG for a different buyer.

Then THE TAKEAWAY. In plain language, finish this sentence for me: "Your
biggest takeaway from this should be..." Three or four lines. Not a
summary of the thread you just wrote. What I should say differently to
this person now that I've seen it, and what I should stop saying.

STEP 3. STOP HERE.
Tell me which one of the five you'd bet on as the closest match, and why
in two lines. Then ask me which one I want to build on, and wait for my
answer. Do not continue until I tell you.

STEP 4. Only after I answer.
Write one ordinary Tuesday in my buyer's life, built on the character I
chose. Not a dramatic day. A forgettable one.

The thing they want but would never say and the thing they fear but would
never admit should press on the whole day without ever being named.

Timestamp it. At least 4 of these hours: the first minute awake, the
shower, the walk to the desk, the ten minutes after something goes well,
the moment they pick up their phone for no reason, the drive home, 9pm,
the last thought before sleep.

Show what they do, not what they feel. No interior narration explaining
the emotion. Put it in an action, a delay, a reread, a tab left open. One
thing they almost do and don't. One thing they say that isn't what they
mean. No epiphany. Nothing resolves. It's a Tuesday. Under 700 words.

STEP 5. Then:
1. THE DIVERGENCE. Line up the Tuesday my avatar doc implied in Step 1
   against the one you just wrote. The three moments where they differ
   most, and which part of the character insight caused each change.
2. THE HOUR I GOT WRONG. The single moment my avatar doc could not have
   predicted, and what that says about what I've been assuming.
3. THE WORDS. 10 phrases from this day this person would plausibly type
   into a search bar, a group chat, or a 2am forum post. Their words.
   Give me all ten together, in one block, numbered 1 to 10, with nothing
   in between them and no commentary. The next prompt takes that block as
   a single paste, so it has to survive one copy.
4. Mark anything you invented that isn't supported by what I gave you.
5. WHERE TO GO NEXT. I'm now holding the common thread and the day. Give
   me 3 questions worth running from here. Each one a question this
   material opened and did not answer, not a generic research question.
   For each: the question in one line, what it would tell me that I don't
   already have, and the one line I'd paste to start it. Best first.
```

## Adjacent Territories

*Stage 2. Where this person talks without your vocabulary.*

```
I need to find where my target buyers discuss their problems online,
knowing they won't be using my terminology. Help me map adjacent
territories.

MY CORE PROBLEM: [paste]
MY TARGET BUYER: [paste]
THE CHARACTER INSIGHT: [paste the "wants but would never say" line]
THEIR TEN PHRASES: [paste the 10 phrases from the previous prompt]

Identify:

1. COMMUNITY ADJACENCIES. Where do these people gather online?
   Subreddits with estimated size, forums, LinkedIn groups, review sites,
   Q and A sites, Discord servers, Facebook groups.

2. BOOK ADJACENCIES. What nonfiction do they read when this problem
   bites? I'll mine the reviews, so favour books with hundreds of them.

3. FICTION ADJACENCIES. What novels, memoirs, or shows portray their
   life? People reveal themselves in the reviews of stories about them.
   Where do readers discuss these?

4. EMOTIONAL ADJACENCIES. What would they search when frustrated,
   ashamed, or hopeful about this problem? Use their ten phrases as the
   starting vocabulary, not mine.

5. FAILURE ADJACENCIES. What have they tried that didn't work, and where
   do they complain about it?

Rules:
- No source where the main activity is people selling to each other. I
  want rooms where nobody is pitching.
- At least two sources I would never have thought to check. If everything
  on the list is obvious, you haven't gone far enough out.

Output a prioritized research plan: top 5 communities, top 5 books or
shows to mine reviews from, top 10 search queries written the way my
buyer would type them, top 5 emotional phrases to look for.

Then: which single source would you start with, and what do you expect to
find there that the others won't have?
```

## The Mining Sprint

*Stage 3. Raw verbatim language from one chosen source.*

```
Research the following source to extract raw customer language about
[PROBLEM].

SOURCE TO MINE: [your chosen source from the map]
SEARCH QUERIES: [3 queries from your map]

Extract:
1. VERBATIM PAIN QUOTES. Exact phrases, including typos and bad grammar.
2. EMOTIONAL LANGUAGE. Frustration, fear, shame, hope.
3. METAPHORS. How they picture the problem.
4. QUESTIONS. The exact questions that reveal their mental model.
5. FAILED SOLUTIONS. What they tried, and how they describe the failure.

FORMAT for each:
Quote: "[exact quote]"
Source: [specific location, close enough that I could find it again]
Category: [Pain / Emotion / Metaphor / Question / Failed Solution]
Usability: [headline, email, opening, FAQ, objection]

Minimum 8 quotes. Prioritize emotional charge and vivid language.

Rules:
- If a quote reads like marketing copy, you are mining the wrong layer.
  Go to the comments, the one-star reviews, the 2am vent posts.
- At least two quotes should be uncomfortable to read.
- Do not clean anything up. Do not summarize a quote into a theme. The
  raw string is the asset.
- If you cannot find real quotes for a category, say so and leave it
  empty. Do not compose an example to fill the slot.

Then: the one quote you would build a whole page around, and why that one.
```

## The Cross-Check

*Stage 4. Fiction against real language. Find the misdiagnosis.*

```
Analyze these quotes and find the hidden patterns.

RAW QUOTES: [paste your mining results]
THE CHARACTER INSIGHT: [paste the common thread from the casting]
WHAT I BELIEVED ABOUT MY AUDIENCE BEFORE TODAY: [1 to 2 sentences,
written before you started, uncleaned]

Give me:

1. THE MISDIAGNOSIS PATTERN
   - What do they THINK their problem is?
   - What is their problem ACTUALLY?
   - Why do they miss it? Be specific about the mechanism, not "lack of
     awareness."

2. LANGUAGE TRANSLATION TABLE
   | What they say | What it means | My term for it |
   Minimum 5 rows. Column one must be quoted, not paraphrased.

3. THE CROSS-CHECK
   Compare today's quotes against the character insight. Where does the
   timeless want show up in this week's language? Quote both sides of
   each overlap. Where the two layers agree, that is the message: deep
   enough to last, current enough to sound native.
   Then the disagreements. Where does the literature say one thing and
   the live language say another? Do not resolve it. A gap between the
   two is usually two different buyers wearing the same job title.

4. THE "NOBODY IS SAYING THIS" OPPORTUNITY
   What truth sits in this data that nobody in my market is articulating?
   Then tell me why they aren't. If it is because it doesn't sell, say
   that.

5. THE CORRECTION
   Put my prior belief next to what the data says. Name the one specific
   way I was wrong. Do not soften it.
```

## Casting Call

*Fallback. No avatar doc? Run this first.*

```
You're going to interview me and then write four short things I need.

Do not write them yet. Do not guess at my answers. Interview me first.

WHAT I SELL: [one line. What you do, and who pays you for it.]

ASK THIS AS YOUR VERY FIRST QUESTION, BEFORE ANYTHING ELSE

"In one or two sentences, what do you believe about your buyer right now?
Who are they and what do they want? Don't think about it too hard."

Save my answer word for word. Do not improve it, correct it, or comment
on it. Do not react to it at all. Just store it and move on. I am going
to compare it against something later, so it has to be my raw belief from
before this conversation changed it.

THE RULES FOR THE REST OF THE INTERVIEW

Ask me ONE question at a time and wait for my answer before the next.
No more than eight questions after the first one.
Base every question on what I just told you, not on a script.
If an answer of mine is vague, push once. Ask for the specific number,
the specific person, or the specific sentence they said. Then move on.
Somewhere in there, ask which one thing I am currently stuck on: a sales
page, a presentation, an email sequence, or my content.

WHAT TO ASK ME ABOUT

Anchor the interview in real people I have actually worked with, not a
type of person I imagine. Get me to describe two or three actual past
clients from memory. Then dig for:

- What their business actually looks like: revenue range, team size, how
  long they have been at it
- What was going on in their business the week they decided to hire me.
  Not the reason they gave. What was actually happening.
- What they had already tried that didn't work, and how they talked about
  that failure
- The words they used when they first described the problem to me, as
  close to verbatim as I can remember
- What a bad Tuesday looks like for this person, hour by hour
- What they were privately afraid of that they never said out loud
- What they would have to admit about themselves for my solution to be
  the obvious answer

IF I TELL YOU I DON'T HAVE CLIENTS YET

Switch approach. Ask who I have had the conversation with. A prospect who
said no, someone at a conference, a former colleague, the version of me
from three years ago. Real remembered humans only. Same questions. Never
let me answer with a market segment.

THEN WRITE THIS, AND NOTHING ELSE

1. PROBLEM STATEMENT
One sentence, in the words my buyer would use, not my industry's. It
should name a situation, not a category.
Good: "Their proposals stall for three weeks and they don't know if it's
the price or the pitch."
Bad: "I help businesses optimize their sales process."

2. BUYER DESCRIPTION
One paragraph, 80 to 120 words, written as one specific person. It must
contain their revenue range and team size, how long they have been doing
this, what a bad Tuesday actually looks like, what they have already
tried that failed, the thing they want that they would never say out
loud, and at least one phrase in their own words.
Write it as though describing someone you have met. No adjectives that
could apply to any business owner. If "ambitious," "motivated,"
"growth-minded," or "looking to scale" appear anywhere, delete them and
write what is underneath.

3. WHAT I BELIEVED BEFORE TODAY
Print my answer to your very first question back to me, word for word,
exactly as I said it. Do not clean it up. If it was vague, leave it
vague. This is a before picture and it only works untouched.

4. MY STUCK POINT
One line, plus the one sentence of context you got about why.

5. THE SHARPNESS CHECK
Then check your own work and tell me honestly:
Could this buyer description describe more than one kind of person? If
yes, say so and name the sentence doing the least work.
Which single detail here is the one nobody else in my market would know?
What did I not tell you that you needed?
```
